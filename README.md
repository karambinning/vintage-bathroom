# A Vintage Bathroom

Five design schemes for a family bathroom makeover, worked up from one brief:

> 1950s style vintage bathroom, with Victorian style toilet with pull chain, rolltop bath,
> glass shower screen with black beading, mosaic floor, black fittings, large deep wash basin,
> black iron radiator, chandelier, rounded mirror with wall mounted lights either side,
> brass fixtures.

**→ [View the lookbook](https://karambinning.github.io/vintage-bathroom/)**

## What's real and what isn't

- **The room measurements are real.** 5.69 m² of usable floor, 1.84 m across the bath wall,
  2.63 m deep, 2.365 m ceiling, and an L-shaped plan. They were taken from wall planes in a
  Polycam 3D scan of the actual room rather than estimated. The bath currently in the room
  measures exactly 1700 × 700 mm, which is how we know the scan's scale is trustworthy.
- **The five images are AI-generated visualisations, not photographs.** They are style
  references drawn to the brief. They are also drawn more generously than the real room —
  treat them as the feeling, and the measurements as the fact.
- **The costs are planning ranges** for a UK job outside London in 2026. They will move with
  specification and whoever quotes.

## Two things the research turned up

1. **The brief is Victorian, not 1950s.** Pull-chain cisterns, roll-top baths, geometric mosaic
   and brass crossheads are roughly 1837–1910. A genuine 1950s bathroom is mint and pink
   pastels, chrome, checkerboard and a built-in bath. Scheme 05 shows the real 1950s version so
   the choice can be made knowingly.
2. **A roll-top and a shower screen conflict.** A true freestanding roll-top wants 150–200 mm
   clear on every side and a screen cannot seal against a curved rolled rim. In a 1.84 m alcove
   a 1700 mm roll-top leaves about 70 mm a side. The fix is a back-to-wall "shower" roll-top
   with one flat side, or a 1500–1600 mm tub.

## Technical

external requests, no build step and no dependencies — open `index.html` anywhere.
